Our support

Mental health supported living in Nottinghamshire.

We are registered with the Care Quality Commission to support adults living with mental health conditions in their own homes across Nottinghamshire.

Make a referral Talk to us

Clinical understanding is built in, not bought in

This is the part of our service we would ask a commissioner to look at hardest.

Our Nominated Individual — the person accountable to the Care Quality Commission for how this service is run — is Dr Bal Powar, an NHS consultant psychiatrist. He has worked for the NHS since 2000 and has been a consultant psychiatrist since 2011.

His clinical background spans:

  • acute inpatient, community and crisis services
  • community forensic mental health services
  • low secure inpatient services
  • locked rehabilitation services
  • inpatient brain injury services
  • autism and neurodevelopmental NHS community services
  • Care and Treatment Reviews

He studied Medicine at St Bartholomew’s Medical School, has been a member of the Royal College of Psychiatrists since 2006, and gained a distinction in Clinical Neuropsychiatry at Birmingham Medical School in 2018.

What that means practically is that clinical thinking sits inside how this service is led — not brought in from outside once something has already gone wrong. When a support plan needs to account for medication, diagnosis, risk or a person’s history of admission, that understanding is already in the room.

Recovery-focused, and honest about what that means

Support is recovery-focused: the aim is for the person to build a life they actually want, with support scaled to what is genuinely needed at the time — up to and including 24 hours a day.

Recovery does not mean a straight line, and we do not pretend it does. People have better months and worse months. A service that only works when someone is well is not a service. The point of scaling support is that it can go up again when it needs to, without that being treated as a failure or a reason to end a placement.

Your home stays yours

People with mental health conditions lose housing at the point they can least afford to lose it — often during an admission. In supported living, the tenancy and the support are separate arrangements, so a person’s home does not depend on who provides their care, or on them being well.

We work with Beacon Supported Housing CIC to source and adapt homes, and we subscribe to the Real Tenancy Test and REACH Tenancy Standards. See Property Solutions.

Dual diagnosis and overlapping needs

We are registered to support adults with a learning disability and/or autism as well as adults with mental health conditions, and in practice these overlap often. A person can be autistic and unwell at the same time, and be badly served by a service that only understands one of those things.

Where behaviour is part of the picture, our approach is Positive Behaviour Support.

Being straight with you
