Privacy Policy

How we collect, use, store and protect personal information — and what your rights are under UK data protection law.

Working draft — review before publishing

This is a complete draft privacy notice, adapted from the standard structure for a UK health and social care provider and populated with Obsidian’s own details.

It is ready to read, but it is not yet an approved policy. A handful of details only Obsidian can confirm are marked [CONFIRM] — chiefly the ICO registration number, whether a Data Protection Officer is appointed, and the retention schedule. Have it reviewed by whoever is responsible for data protection (and, ideally, a solicitor) before it goes live, then remove this box. Obsidian Supported Living (“we”, “us”, “our”) is committed to protecting the privacy of everyone whose personal information we hold. This policy explains how we handle information about people who contact us or enquire, families and carers, the professionals we work with, people who apply for jobs, and visitors to this website. It covers what we collect, why, who we share it with, how long we keep it and your rights under the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018.

If you receive support from us, a separate notice applies to your care records.

The Service User Privacy Notice explains how we look after the information of the people we support — including health and care records, your NHS number, and how we involve families, advocates and legal representatives. This page covers everyone else.

What personal data we collect

The information we collect depends on your relationship with us.

People we support. We hold detailed care records, including health and other special category data. How we handle these is explained in full in our separate Service User Privacy Notice.

Families, carers and next of kin. Name, relationship to the person we support, and contact details.

Professionals and commissioners. Name, job title, organisation and contact details, and the information you share with us as part of a referral or placement.

Job applicants and staff. Contact details, employment history, references, right-to-work documents, and the results of pre-employment checks including Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) checks. For staff we also hold payroll, training and HR records.

Website visitors. Any information you choose to send us through the contact or application forms, and limited technical information — see “Website data and forms” below.

Why we use your data and our lawful basis

We only use your personal information where the law allows. Our lawful bases under Article 6 of the UK GDPR are usually:

  • Legal obligation — to meet our duties under health and social care law — including the Care Act 2014, the Health and Social Care Act 2008, the Mental Capacity Act 2005, the Mental Health Act 1983 and the Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006 — and our conditions of registration with the Care Quality Commission.
  • Legitimate interests — to run our service safely and effectively, respond to enquiries and referrals, and recruit staff, where doing so does not override your rights.
  • Contract — to deliver the support agreed in a placement, and to manage employment.
  • Vital interests — in an emergency, to protect someone’s life.

For special category data (such as health and disability information), we additionally rely on Article 9(2)(h) — the provision of health and social care — and on Article 9(2)(b) for employment purposes. Where we process data for safeguarding, we rely on the substantial public interest condition in the Data Protection Act 2018.

[CONFIRM] Consent is rarely the correct lawful basis for care records, and is not used here for that reason. Please have a data protection professional confirm this mapping of purposes to lawful bases reflects how Obsidian actually operates.

Who we share your data with

We do not sell your personal information and we never share it for marketing. We share it only where necessary to deliver or oversee your care, meet a legal duty, or keep people safe. Depending on your circumstances, recipients may include:

  • Local authorities and the NHS, including Integrated Care Boards (ICBs), who commission or are involved in your care;
  • GPs, hospitals, community health teams and other health and social care professionals;
  • The Care Quality Commission, as our regulator;
  • Beacon Supported Housing CIC and other housing partners, in relation to your tenancy (kept separate from your support — see our Property Solutions page);
  • Local authority safeguarding teams and, where appropriate, the police;
  • The Disclosure and Barring Service, for staff recruitment;
  • Our professional advisers and service providers — for example IT, payroll and record-keeping systems — who act as our processors under a written contract.

We keep your information within the UK. We do not transfer personal data outside the UK. [CONFIRM] if any supplier you use stores data overseas, this section must be updated and appropriate safeguards described.

How long we keep your data

We keep personal information only for as long as we need it. Enquiries are kept while we are dealing with them and for a short period afterwards; records of unsuccessful job applicants are kept for six to twelve months. Retention of the care records of people we support is covered in the Service User Privacy Notice. When information is no longer needed, we dispose of it securely.

How we keep your data safe

We use appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect your information against loss, misuse and unauthorised access. These include restricting access to those who need it, staff training in data protection and confidentiality, secure storage of paper and electronic records, and written agreements with the suppliers who process data on our behalf. If a personal data breach occurs that is likely to risk your rights, we will report it to the Information Commissioner’s Office within 72 hours where required, and tell you if you are likely to be significantly affected.

Your rights

Under data protection law you have the right to:

  • be told how your information is used (this notice);
  • ask for a copy of the information we hold about you (a “subject access request”);
  • ask us to correct information that is wrong or incomplete;
  • ask us to delete information, or restrict how we use it, in certain circumstances;
  • object to certain uses of your information;
  • ask us to transfer your information to another organisation, where this applies;
  • not be subject to decisions made solely by automated means — we do not make any such decisions.

Some of these rights are limited where we have a legal duty to keep information, as is often the case with care records. To exercise any right, contact us using the details above. We will respond within one month. You can ask someone — a family member, advocate or friend — to help you, and we can provide this notice in easy read or another format on request.

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your information, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO), Wycliffe House, Water Lane, Wilmslow, Cheshire, SK9 5AF — telephone 0303 123 1113, or ico.org.uk. We would ask that you raise it with us first so we can try to put things right.

Website data and forms

When you send us a message through the contact form or apply through the careers form on this website, we collect the details you provide (such as your name, email address, phone number, and anything you write or attach) so that we can respond to you. This information is sent to our enquiries or recruitment inbox and is not used for any other purpose. This website uses Google reCAPTCHA to reduce spam, which is subject to Google’s own privacy policy and terms.
