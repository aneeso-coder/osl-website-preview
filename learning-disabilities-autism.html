Our support

Learning disability and autism support in Nottinghamshire.

We are registered with the Care Quality Commission to provide personal care to adults with a learning disability and/or autism — both under and over 65 — in their own homes.

Make a referral Talk to us

Communication comes first

Before anything else works, we have to understand how someone communicates, and make sure they can understand us. Everything else in a support plan is built on top of that, and when it is missing, everything else fails.

That might mean symbols, objects of reference, signing, a communication app, or simply knowing that a particular person needs quiet and time before answering. We adapt to the person’s communication. We do not ask them to adapt to ours.

It is worth being blunt about why this matters. A great deal of what gets recorded as “behaviour that challenges” is a person with no reliable way to say no, or to say that hurts, or to say I have had enough of this. Get the communication right and a surprising amount of the rest gets easier.

Autism-informed, not autism-adjacent

Sensory environment, predictability and processing time are not extras to be added once the “real” support is in place. They are the difference between a placement that works and one that breaks down.

In practice that means paying attention to the things that are easy to dismiss: lighting, noise, smell, texture, how many people are in a room, how much warning someone gets before something changes, and how long they are given to process a question before someone asks it again in different words.

Who leads this at Obsidian

Our Registered Manager, Dawn Newby, holds a Postgraduate Certificate in Autism and has worked in social care for more than 25 years, starting as a support worker alongside people with intellectual disabilities.

Our Nominated Individual, Dr Bal Powar, is an NHS consultant psychiatrist whose background includes autism and neurodevelopmental NHS community services and Care and Treatment Reviews. He is also the parent of an adult with a severe learning disability and autism — Obsidian was established out of that experience.

That combination is unusual, and it is the reason this organisation exists. Read more about the team.

Health, epilepsy and staying well

People with a learning disability die younger than they should, often of things that were treatable. Good support means noticing changes early and making sure health appointments actually happen and are understood.

Epilepsy is common alongside a learning disability, so we treat it as core knowledge rather than a specialism. Dr Powar is a certified trainer in epilepsy awareness and buccal rescue medication administration, and both he and Dawn are accredited to train staff in CPR, first aid, choking response and safe defibrillator use. That training is delivered in-house, which means it is shaped around the people we actually support and can be refreshed quickly when someone’s needs change.

When behaviour is part of the picture

Where someone’s behaviour is described as challenging, our approach is Positive Behaviour Support — understanding what the behaviour is achieving for the person, changing the environment rather than the person, and reducing restrictive practice over time.

The right home matters too

For a lot of people we support, the building is not a background detail — it is half the problem or half the solution. Through our housing partner Beacon Supported Housing CIC we source and adapt properties around the person who will live in them, with the tenancy always kept separate from the support.
