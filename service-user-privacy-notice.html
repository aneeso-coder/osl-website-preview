Service User Privacy Notice

This notice is for the people we support and their families. It explains, in plain language, what information we keep about you, why we keep it, who we share it with and what your rights are.

Working draft — review before publishing

This is a complete draft notice, adapted from the standard structure for a UK adult social care provider and populated with Obsidian’s own details.

It is ready to read, but not yet approved. A few details only Obsidian can confirm are marked [CONFIRM] — chiefly the ICO registration number, the data protection lead, and the retention schedule. Have it reviewed by whoever is responsible for data protection before it goes live. If you receive support from Obsidian Supported Living, we keep information about you so that we can support you safely and well. Looking after that information properly is part of looking after you. This notice explains how we do that, under the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018. If it would help, ask a member of staff, a family member or an advocate to go through it with you, or ask us for it in easy read.

What information we hold about you

To assess your needs, plan and deliver your support, keep you safe and meet our legal duties, we hold information such as:

  • your name, date of birth, address, NHS number and (where relevant) National Insurance number;
  • your care and support plans, risk assessments and the daily records staff keep;
  • information about your health, disability, mental health and medication;
  • where relevant to your care, information about your race, ethnicity, religion or beliefs, and sexual orientation. Under the UK GDPR this is called special category data and is given extra protection;
  • details of the people involved in your life — family, next of kin, and anyone legally involved in your affairs, such as an attorney under a Lasting or Enduring Power of Attorney, a deputy appointed by the Court of Protection, or an appointee who helps manage your money;
  • financial information, including bank details, where we support you with money or act as your appointee;
  • information relating to safeguarding, where there is a concern about your safety or wellbeing.

Why we use your information, and our lawful basis

We only use your information where the law allows. Our main lawful bases under Article 6 of the UK GDPR are:

  • Public task (Article 6(1)(e)) — providing social care is a task carried out in the public interest;
  • Legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c)) — to meet our duties under laws including the Care Act 2014, the Health and Social Care Act 2008, the Mental Capacity Act 2005, the Mental Health Act 1983 and the Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006, and our conditions of registration with the Care Quality Commission;
  • Vital interests (Article 6(1)(d)) — in an emergency, to protect your life.

Because your care information includes health and other special category data, we also rely on Article 9(2)(h) of the UK GDPR — the provision of health and social care. For safeguarding, we rely on the substantial public interest condition in the Data Protection Act 2018. We do not rely on your consent to keep your care records, so you do not have to worry that withdrawing consent would leave us unable to support you safely. We only ask for your consent for things that are genuinely optional, such as using your story or photograph in our materials.

If you cannot make a decision yourself

Sometimes a person may not be able to make a particular decision about their information at a particular time. Where that happens, we follow the Mental Capacity Act 2005: we assume you can make your own decisions unless it is shown otherwise, we support you to be as involved as possible, and where a decision has to be made for you it is made in your best interests. We will involve the people who have a legal right to be involved — such as an attorney or Court of Protection deputy — and you have the right to support from an advocate, including an Independent Mental Capacity Advocate where one applies.

Who we share your information with

We do not sell your information and we never share it for marketing. We share it only where it is needed to support you, to meet a legal duty, or to keep you or others safe. Depending on your situation, we may share information with:

  • the local authority and the NHS, including Integrated Care Boards, who commission or are involved in your care;
  • your GP, hospitals, community nurses and other health and social care professionals;
  • the Care Quality Commission, as our regulator;
  • local authority safeguarding teams and, where necessary, the police and emergency services;
  • Beacon Supported Housing CIC and other housing partners, about your tenancy — kept separate from your support (see Property Solutions);
  • the suppliers who help us keep records securely, who act under a written contract and may only use your information as we instruct.

Your NHS number and the national data opt-out

Like other adult social care providers, we use your NHS number to make sure the right information is matched to the right person when we work with the NHS — for example with your GP, a hospital or a community nurse. If you do not want your NHS number used in this way, you can raise this with your GP.

The NHS also runs a national data opt-out, which lets you choose whether your confidential health and care information is used for research and planning beyond your own care. You can set or change your choice at nhs.uk/your-nhs-data-matters.

How long we keep your information

We keep your information only for as long as we need it, following the statutory retention periods for health and adult social care. As a guide, adult social care records are kept for eight years after the end of your support, and records relating to mental health support for twenty years after the end of your support (or eight years after death). When information is no longer needed, we dispose of it securely.

[CONFIRM] These are the common statutory periods from the Records Management Code of Practice for health and social care; confirm they match your own retention schedule.

How we keep your information safe

We protect your information against loss, misuse and unauthorised access. Only staff who need your information can see it, our staff are trained in confidentiality and data protection, paper and electronic records are stored securely, and the suppliers who handle information for us are bound by written agreements. If something goes wrong in a way that is likely to affect you, we will report it to the Information Commissioner’s Office where required and tell you if you are likely to be significantly affected.

Your rights

You have the right to:

  • be told how your information is used (this notice);
  • ask for a copy of the information we hold about you;
  • ask us to correct anything that is wrong;
  • ask us to delete or restrict information, in certain circumstances;
  • object to certain uses of your information.

Some of these rights are limited where we have a legal duty to keep your care records. You can ask a family member, friend or advocate to help you use your rights. To make a request, contact us using the details above; we will respond within one month, and we can reply in easy read or another format. If you are unhappy with how we have handled your information you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO), Wycliffe House, Water Lane, Wilmslow, Cheshire, SK9 5AF — telephone 0303 123 1113, or ico.org.uk — but we hope you will tell us first so we can put things right.
