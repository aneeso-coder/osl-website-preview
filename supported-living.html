Our support

Supported living in Nottinghamshire.

The person has their own home, on their own tenancy. We provide the support they need inside it — up to 24 hours a day, seven days a week.

What supported living actually means

Supported living is not a care home with a different name. The difference is the tenancy. In a residential service, the roof and the care come from the same organisation, and the two are bound together. In supported living, the person signs their own tenancy, and support is a separate arrangement on top of it.

That separation is the whole point. It means someone’s home does not depend on who provides their care. If the support arrangements change — including if they change away from us — the person still has their home. We think a provider that cannot be sacked without someone losing their house has too much power over that person’s life.

We work with our housing partner, Beacon Supported Housing CIC, to source and adapt properties, and we subscribe to the Real Tenancy Test and the REACH Tenancy Standards. You can read more on our Property Solutions page.

How much support

As much as the person needs, including 24 hours a day, seven days a week, and including waking night support where that is what is required. The hours are built from the person’s assessed needs, not from a package we already had.

Good support should get lighter

Wherever it is possible, we plan from the first day for the person to rely on staff less. Not because it saves money — because independence is the point. A support plan that looks identical after three years is usually a plan that has stopped paying attention.

Where someone will always need 24/7 support, we say so honestly and plan for that instead. Pretending otherwise sets a person up to fail and wastes a commissioner’s time.

What person-centred means in practice

It is the most overused phrase in social care, so here is what we mean by it concretely.

The support plan is built from what the person wants their week to look like — in their words, or in their communication method — rather than from a template with their name at the top. It covers their routines, their food, their music, their faith, their relationships and the things they want to do. And it changes when they change, because people do.

It also means the person has control over the ordinary decisions: what time they get up, who comes into their home, what is in their fridge. Those are not small things. They are most of what a life is made of.

Who we support

We are registered with the Care Quality Commission to provide personal care in supported living settings to adults with a learning disability and/or autism, and to adults living with mental health conditions — both under and over 65, across Nottinghamshire.

Where behaviour is part of the picture, our approach is Positive Behaviour Support.

Being straight with you

We are registered with the Care Quality Commission. Our service has not yet been inspected, so it does not have a CQC rating. We will link to our report here whatever it says. View our CQC location record.
